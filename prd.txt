1. Executive Summary and Product Decisions
The recommended product is a hyperlocal multi-vendor marketplace for packaged and filtered drinking water. A customer chooses an approved nearby shop, places a one-time or scheduled order, returns empty containers where applicable, and receives delivery from the supplier's own delivery operation. The platform earns commission and/or service fees and controls trust, discovery, transaction records and support.
1.1 Product vision
Make reliable drinking-water ordering as simple as ordering food, while giving local water suppliers a lightweight digital storefront, order book and returnable-container ledger.
1.2 Problems being solved
Customers often depend on calls or WhatsApp, cannot compare nearby shops, and have weak visibility into delivery status or water-quality evidence.
Suppliers receive unstructured orders, lose track of empty jars/deposits, cannot measure repeat demand, and struggle to reach new local customers.
The marketplace operator needs supplier verification, clear order evidence, configurable commission, dispute handling and trustworthy performance data.
1.3 Recommended decisions
Decision

1.4 Success definition for the pilot
At least 15–30 verified suppliers in one tightly defined Karachi launch zone before public marketing.
A customer can find a serviceable shop, understand the empty-bottle rule, place COD order and receive delivery without calling support.
A supplier can accept, prepare, dispatch and complete an order from the app in under one minute of active interaction.
Admin can trace every price, status change, payment, container adjustment and user action involved in a dispute.
Pilot targets: supplier acceptance above 85%, fulfillment above 90%, cancellation below 10%, and container discrepancy below 3%. Targets are initial hypotheses to be validated.

2.2 Product components
Mobile app: single Android/iOS codebase in TypeScript. Role selection controls navigation and feature permissions, but backend authorization remains the source of truth.
Laravel API: authentication, geospatial shop discovery, catalog, pricing quote, orders, payments, containers, notifications, settlements and reports.
Admin portal: responsive Laravel web application for approval, operations, finance, content, support, configuration and analytics.
Background workers: push/SMS/email, supplier-acceptance timers, recurring order generation, document-expiry reminders, payment webhooks, exports and settlement jobs.
Third-party services: maps/geocoding, SMS/OTP, Firebase Cloud Messaging, object storage/CDN, email and payment service provider.
2.3 Role switching in one app
A new user chooses Customer or Register as Supplier. Every valid supplier account may also use Customer mode.
Supplier mode remains locked while application status is Draft, Under Review, Changes Required, Rejected, Suspended or Expired.
If the same phone number owns both roles, Profile contains a clear Switch to Customer / Switch to Supplier action.
Deep links and notifications must open the correct role and screen only after authorization is confirmed.
Role state must never be trusted from local storage alone; fetch the current role and permissions after login and app resume.
3. Business Model and Marketplace Rules
3.1 Value to each side
Customer value
 | Supplier value
 | Platform value
 | Nearby verified shops, transparent rates, repeat ordering, delivery status and container record
 | New local customers, structured orders, digital catalog, delivery control and sales reports
 | Commission/service revenue, supplier network, data, standards and customer trust
 | 
3.2 Revenue options
Commission per completed order: recommended starting model. Configure by supplier/category; charge only on eligible components such as product subtotal.
Customer service fee: small fixed or percentage fee shown before checkout. Use carefully because low-ticket water orders are price-sensitive.
Delivery fee share: supplier may retain all delivery fee or share a configured percentage if the platform later manages riders.
Supplier subscription plan: Phase 3 option for premium placement, analytics, more branches or lower commission.
Sponsored placement: clearly labeled promoted shops/products; organic ranking must remain trustworthy.
3.3 Marketplace rules
Only admin-approved shops appear in discovery. Suspended or document-expired shops stop receiving new orders but retain historical access.
Each order belongs to one supplier branch, one delivery address, one currency (PKR) and one immutable pricing snapshot.
Supplier controls item price, stock, delivery hours, minimum order and service area within admin limits.
Platform controls allowed categories, commission, fee rules, cancellation policy, quality-document rules and moderation.
Default ranking should consider serviceability, open status, recent fulfillment, ETA, rating and distance—not price alone.
Customer phone/address is shared with a supplier only after order placement and only for fulfillment/support purposes.

4.3 Application lifecycle
Draft – supplier can save and continue later.
Submitted – fields are locked except contacts until reviewer action.
Under review – compliance team verifies identity, location, documents and catalog.
Changes required – reviewer identifies exact missing/invalid items; supplier resubmits.
Approved – shop can finish catalog and activate after payout and operating settings are valid.
Rejected – reason and appeal route recorded; repeated applications remain linked.
Suspended – shop hidden for policy, quality, fraud or performance issue; active-order handling defined.
Expired – one or more mandatory documents expired; new orders paused until renewal approval.
4.4 Ongoing compliance controls
Automated reminders 30, 14, 7 and 1 day before required document expiry.
Admin dashboard for expiring, expired, rejected and never-reviewed evidence.
Version history: previous file, new file, uploader, reviewer, decision time and notes remain auditable.
Quality badge shows only the verified evidence type and valid-through date; never imply platform laboratory certification.
Periodic spot checks and complaint-triggered re-review; admin may pause only affected products or the whole shop.
PCRWR/authority report checks may be used by operations as an additional signal, not as the only supplier decision.

6. End-to-End Workflows
6.1 Customer one-time order
Customer selects/adds an address; backend geocodes and validates serviceability.
Backend returns only approved, active and currently serviceable shops; default discovery radius is 5 km.
Customer opens one shop, adds items and states how many valid empty containers will be returned.
Backend generates a quote: item snapshot, deposit/shortage, delivery, service fee, discount and total; quote has an expiry time.
Customer selects COD (MVP) and confirms. Client sends an idempotency key so double taps cannot create duplicate orders.
Supplier receives a high-priority alert and accepts or rejects within the configured timer.
Supplier marks Preparing, then Out for Delivery. Customer receives status notifications.
At delivery, supplier records actual valid empties, collects/returns any difference, and enters customer delivery OTP or proof.
Backend completes the order, updates container and financial ledgers, creates earnings/commission entries, and enables reorder/review.
6.2 Supplier application
Create account with phone OTP and choose supplier type.
Enter owner/business identity and place shop GPS pin.
Upload required documents; fields adapt to supplier type and city.
Configure hours, service area, fee, minimum order and payout details.
Add at least one valid product with price, container type and deposit/exchange rule.
Submit and receive an immutable submission reference.
Admin checks evidence and either requests precise changes, rejects, or approves.
After approval and catalog readiness, supplier activates the shop and receives a test-order checklist.
6.3 Recurring delivery (Phase 2)
Customer selects shop, products, frequency (for example weekly), preferred day/slot, start date and empty-container expectation.
Subscription is a schedule template; every occurrence creates a normal order with a fresh price/stock quote and unique payment state.
Customer can skip the next occurrence, pause, resume or cancel before a cutoff. Supplier cannot silently change the schedule.
If shop is closed/out of stock, notify both sides and request customer action; do not auto-substitute without explicit consent.
Online payment failure produces Payment Required and retry flow; it must not create a fulfillable order until policy conditions are satisfied.
6.4 Cancellation, rejection and refund
Before supplier acceptance: customer may cancel; online authorization is voided/refunded automatically where supported.
After acceptance but before dispatch: configurable cancellation; customer chooses reason and may incur a clearly disclosed fee only if legally/commercially approved.
After Out for Delivery: normal cancellation disabled; support-led exception with reason and evidence.
Supplier rejection requires reason (out of stock, capacity, address, closed, other). Repeated rejection affects ranking/performance.
All refunds have requested, processing, succeeded and failed states; an order must not be labeled Refunded solely because a request was created.

7. Empty Bottle / Jar Deposit System
Returnable containers are a first-class inventory and financial feature. The system must know which supplier, brand and size owns each liability. A generic 'empty bottle: yes/no' field is not sufficient.
7.1 Container modes
Mode
 | Checkout behavior
 | Delivery behavior
 | Non-returnable
 | No empty or deposit question
 | Normal item delivery
 | Exchange required
 | Customer declares same valid empty quantity
 | Shortfall becomes configured deposit/charge or order exception
 | Refundable deposit
 | New container adds deposit; customer may return later
 | Valid return creates deposit credit/refund according to policy
 | Refill-only
 | Requires eligible customer-owned/recorded container
 | Supplier validates container; rejected/damaged container policy applies
 | 
7.2 Data tracked per transaction
Container type: size/capacity, brand compatibility, supplier ownership and deposit value.
Ordered full quantity, expected empty return, actual valid empty return, damaged/rejected quantity and shortage.
Deposit charged, deposit returned/credited, adjustment reason, actor and proof/photo if disputed.
Customer container balance and supplier container inventory/receivable by container type.
The exact deposit rate snapshot at order time; later rate changes must not rewrite history.
7.3 Recommended rules
Default: accept an empty only for the same supplier/brand/size compatibility group. Cross-brand exchange requires explicit admin configuration.
Customer may declare fewer empties than ordered; quote immediately adds deposit for the shortfall.
Actual delivery quantity overrides declared quantity only after supplier records it and customer receives a receipt/notification.
Manual ledger adjustments require a reason. Above a configured PKR/quantity threshold, admin approval is required.
A returned deposit is a financial event and must link to payment/refund/credit evidence—not only change a container count.
Disputed or damaged empties remain in a Pending Review state until resolved; do not silently charge the customer.

8.2 Pricing formula
Customer total: Product subtotal + delivery fee + platform service fee + container deposit/shortage + applicable tax − item/coupon discounts − eligible credits.
 | 
Supplier net: Eligible product revenue + supplier-owned delivery revenue − marketplace commission − supplier-funded discounts − refunds/adjustments ± settlement corrections. Deposits remain a separate liability/ledger component.
 | 
Backend is authoritative for price and fee calculation. Mobile submits item IDs/quantities and displays the signed/versioned quote response.
Order items store product name, SKU, price, deposit, tax and discount snapshots so history survives later catalog edits.
Use integer minor units (paisa) in calculation/storage or a decimal money type; never binary floating-point for financial totals.
Quote expires (for example, after 10 minutes). Checkout must revalidate stock, hours, serviceability and total.

8.4 Supplier settlement
Every completed order creates immutable ledger entries; a settlement groups eligible entries for a defined period.
Recommended pilot: weekly settlement with a 2–3 day dispute buffer; actual policy depends on payment and commercial model.
Payout states: Draft, Approved, Processing, Paid, Failed, Held, Reversed. Paid requires reference and date.
Changing payout account requires OTP and admin re-verification; apply a temporary payout hold after change.
Statement shows order IDs, gross, commission, delivery, discounts, refunds, adjustments, deposits/liability and net payout.

10.3 Geospatial approach
Store normalized latitude/longitude plus a geospatial point for every address and supplier branch.
Discovery: database spatial/radius query returns candidate active shops within the configured straight-line radius.
Serviceability: check supplier polygon/radius, operating hours, capacity, minimum order and product availability.
Final ETA/delivery fee: use configured zone or route distance; call Routes API only where it changes the decision, then cache briefly.
Never expose unrestricted Maps server keys in the mobile bundle. Apply application restrictions, API restrictions and quotas.
10.4 API and event conventions
Version every public endpoint under /api/v1 and use consistent JSON success/error envelopes.
Use request validation, policies/permissions and resource serialization. Client-hidden buttons are not authorization.
Cursor pagination for product/order/inbox feeds; Laravel documents it as suitable for large data sets and infinite scroll [S11].
Idempotency-Key required for order creation, online payment initiation, webhook processing, refunds and manual adjustments.
Create domain events after database commit (OrderPlaced, OrderAccepted, DeliveryCompleted, PaymentSucceeded, ContainerAdjusted).
Outbox/retry pattern recommended for critical integrations so a database commit cannot silently lose its notification or payment event.
10.5 Performance-aware mobile implementation
Home/product APIs must be paginated; do not return the entire catalog. Initial page target: 15–20 items.
Serve a listing thumbnail around 400×400 in WebP/AVIF, normally under 100–150 KB; detail image around 1000 px, normally under 250 KB. Validate with real content and quality needs.
Use CDN URLs and responsive variants; never send Base64 images or supplier documents inside normal JSON responses.
Stable list keys, memoized product cards, shallow render tree and image caching. Keep pricing/container calculation off the list card.
Use API gzip/Brotli where supported, database indexes, response field selection and cached shop summaries.
Measure release builds on low/mid-range Android devices; React Native performance guidance recommends profiling with development mode disabled [S12].

11.1 Critical database constraints
Unique normalized phone and explicit soft-delete/account-state policy.
Money fields non-negative where appropriate; order total equals persisted component sum.
One active cart supplier per customer; one successful payment capture per provider reference/idempotency key.
Valid order-state transitions enforced inside transactional services, not by arbitrary status update endpoints.
Ledger entries are immutable; corrections post reversing/adjusting entries.
Foreign keys/indexes for customer, shop, order state/time, payment reference, document expiry and geospatial point.

12.1 Response and error standard
Success includes data plus meta for pagination/quote/version as applicable.
Validation returns field-level codes/messages; app maps codes to localized copy.
Business errors use stable codes such as SHOP_CLOSED, OUT_OF_STOCK, ADDRESS_NOT_SERVICEABLE, QUOTE_EXPIRED, INVALID_CONTAINER_RETURN and PAYMENT_PENDING.
Every response includes or echoes a request/correlation ID. Logs must not expose OTPs, tokens, CNIC images or payment secrets.
409 for state/idempotency conflicts, 422 for validation/business input, 401/403 for authentication/authorization, 429 for rate limits.
13. Security, Privacy and Compliance
13.1 Authentication and authorization
OTP endpoints: per-phone, per-IP and per-device throttling; cooldown, expiry, maximum attempts and abuse monitoring.
Use short/controlled mobile token lifetime, device name, revoke-on-logout and server-side account-state checks. Store tokens only in OS secure storage.
Admin portal uses secure sessions, CSRF protection, least-privilege RBAC and MFA for finance/super-admin roles.
Laravel policies/permissions protect every supplier-owned resource; a supplier cannot guess another supplier's IDs.
Sensitive actions require step-up verification: payout change, high-value refund, container adjustment, role grant and account deletion.
13.2 Data protection
TLS everywhere; encrypt high-risk fields at rest; secrets in managed environment/secret store, never committed to Git.
CNIC and compliance files use private storage, signed short-lived URLs and explicit viewer permissions; watermark/download logging recommended.
Collect minimum data; define retention for OTP logs, exact location, identity documents, tickets, financial records and deleted accounts.
Do not send CNIC, payment details, full address or access tokens to crash reports/analytics. Redact structured logs.
Provide privacy notice, terms, supplier agreement, cancellation/refund policy, prohibited products/claims and account deletion/contact route.
13.3 Water quality and supplier compliance
PSQCA lists Bottled Drinking Water (PS 4639) among compulsory standards items [S6]; applicability and evidence must be legally reviewed for each supplier type.
Sindh Food Authority's published licensing flow requests identity verification, business details and products/services, plus the applicable fee [S7].
PCRWR publishes quarterly bottled-water monitoring. Its Jan–Mar 2026 report summarized 228 brands sampled and 193 (85%) overall safe, reinforcing the need for evidence and expiry monitoring [S8].
The app should show evidence type, issuer, last verified date and expiry without making unsupported medical or purity claims.
Platform terms must state supplier responsibility for product quality, lawful operation, labeling, packaging, delivery hygiene and recall cooperation.
Legal review required: This specification is product/technical guidance, not legal advice. Before launch, obtain written review for SFA/PSQCA applicability, tax/invoicing, consumer/refund terms, payments, privacy, records, marketing claims and supplier agreements.

14.1 Failure and offline behavior
Browse cached last-known shop/product data with a visible offline/stale indicator; never allow offline final order submission without server confirmation.
After a checkout timeout, query by idempotency key/order lookup before offering Retry to prevent duplicates.
If maps fail, allow manual address entry and store unverified coordinates/status for review; clearly explain serviceability uncertainty.
If push is delayed, in-app polling/resume refresh must still show the correct order state. Push is a hint, not the source of truth.
If payment callback is delayed, display Processing and reconcile asynchronously; never ask the user to pay twice immediately.
